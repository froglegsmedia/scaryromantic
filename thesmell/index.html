Coroner's Case #0447

Every Body Tells a Story. This One Screams.

No wounds. No struggle. No cause of death that makes sense on paper. Just a smell that lingers in the room long after the body's gone cold — and gets worse every time someone tries to explain it away.

The Story

It Doesn't Knock. It Doesn't Chase. It Just Waits For You To Breathe.

LOGLINE: When a disgraced coroner investigates a string of horrific, unexplained fatalities in an isolated mountain town, he discovers victims are being targeted by a predatory entity heralded by an impossibly foul, otherworldly stench — and once that rancid odor hits your lungs, you have mere hours before it brutally claims your life.

Three years removed from the scandal that ended his career, coroner Silas Wren takes the only job left that will have him: filling in for the county examiner in Halloway's Gap, a mountain town of four hundred people that's just recorded its fifth unexplained death in six weeks. No trauma. No toxicology hits. Just a smell in the lungs so foul the responding officers threw up at the scene.

Wren tells himself it's methane, mold, decomposition gas — anything with a name. But the survivors describe the same thing: a wrongness in the air that arrives before anything else does, and once you've smelled it, you're already on the clock. Hours, not days. And it always finds you again.

As Wren digs deeper — through town records, church basements, and the mountain hollow the locals won't name after dark — he realizes Halloway's Gap has been feeding something for a very long time, and the town's silence has always been the price of staying alive. Now the smell is spreading toward the valley, and Wren has to solve what a decade of coroners couldn't: not how these people died, but what's still hungry.

The Threat

It Has No Name. Only A Smell.

"You won't see it coming. You'll just notice you're the only one who can still smell the room."

Nobody in Halloway's Gap has ever seen it and lived long enough to describe it clearly. What they agree on is the smell — rot and ozone and something sweetly wrong underneath, a stench that gets stronger the more attention you pay to it, as if it feeds on being noticed.

It doesn't stalk. It doesn't need to. Once the smell reaches you, you're already marked, and there is nowhere in Halloway's Gap far enough to outrun a scent that's already inside your chest.
