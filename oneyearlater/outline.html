One Year Later

After a botched bank robbery, two lifelong friends spend six years on the run. Every chapter begins exactly one year later, forcing the audience to piece together the missing time as changing identities, broken relationships, and buried betrayals push them toward an inevitable reckoning.

Written by Tyler Forrest

Core characters

Four people

Jack

A charismatic risk-taker addicted to freedom. Impulsive, allergic to ordinary work, and hiding how afraid he is behind the charm. He used to date Claire, and he slept with her after she married Evan. He carries that guilt under a loyalty to his best friend that feels almost suspicious.

Evan

A loyal planner desperate for a life that sits still. Fired just as Claire becomes pregnant, he agrees to the robbery because it looks like the only way to provide. He loves Jack like a brother and blames him for everything they become, until the bond hardens into something else: they will die, and kill, for each other.

Claire

Evan’s wife, living with one drunken mistake with Jack early in the marriage. The question of who fathered her child is never settled. She will not raise a family on the run, and she divorces Evan.

Detective Brown

Recently retired. Recently widowed. Looking for a purpose. He stumbles onto the fugitives by chance and hunts them across borders with no official backing. Sharp enough to connect impossible dots, obsessed enough to look unhinged, and addicted to the chase.

Chapter 01

The Bank Robbery

Small town · Population 108

The chapter opens in the car. Jack and Evan are heading into an off-grid town, and the audience does not yet know they are about to rob a bank. What we feel first is the friendship, Evan’s money trouble, a baby on the way, and a buried fault between Jack and Claire. A black Buick holds its distance in the mirror.

Masks go on. Guns come out. They are amateurs, making it up as they go, and the weapons are loaded with blanks. They barely get away. The robbery car burns in the desert. A second car, stashed for the getaway, disappears with them and the money. Detective Brown works what they left: spent blanks, and one faint clue on the security footage. His partner tells him to let it go. He retires next month.

Chapter 02

One Year Later

The island

False names. A tropical island. For a moment it looks like they won. Jack drinks, flirts, and throws cash around a tiki bar. Evan sits out of the celebration, trying to get back to Claire before she gives birth.

Across the bar, Detective Brown drinks alone. Freshly retired, newly widowed, he hears Jack half-joke about a bank job. He follows them to the duffel bags full of cash and calls an old colleague, who tells him to drop it. Brown will not. The next day Jack starts a drunken beach fight with a pack of frat boys. Local police cuff them, then kick them off the island before the arrest becomes a real case. They take the next boat toward North America. Brown watches from the shore.

Chapter 03

One Year Later

Back home · The boardwalk

The pressure comes from both sides. Jack shakes a black Buick that tails him from a bar. At home, Evan and Claire are tearing apart. Their one-year-old runs straight to Jack when he arrives at the worst moment, and the question of the child’s father sharpens without being answered. Jack warns Evan about the Buick and forces a pistol on him.

Days later the same car follows Evan and Claire to a grocery store. Paranoid, Evan pulls the gun in public. Claire is terrified. Jack finds his window smashed and a note demanding the money. Detective Brown, without a badge, knocks on Claire’s door. She lies to him. He scares her into sending Evan to a motel, and he plants a GPS tracker under the car. The next night Brown breaks into the empty house and finds fake IDs and cash.

Jack sees the Buick outside his place and lures the driver into a boardwalk alley, with Evan as backup. The ambush turns lethal. Jack shoots first and is overpowered. Evan kills the man to save him. In one instant they are murderers, not only bank robbers. Detective Brown arrives, flashes his old badge, and orders them to freeze. They run.

Chapter 04

One Year Later

A remote Canadian cabin

Bearded and off the grid, they can barely keep their own names straight. Evan is divorced. After a payphone call to Claire, he talks about going back for the child and about how loyal she was. The guilt breaks Jack. He confesses that he slept with Claire early in the marriage. The cabin becomes a fistfight. It ends with Evan holding Jack at gunpoint.

Armed trucks pull up outside. The man they killed on the boardwalk was a crime boss’s son, and the father has found them. He wants revenge and he wants the cash. Jack and Evan fight the siege together, take the boss down, and bargain the money’s location with the men who are left. The bag they are led to is rigged. It erupts with a blinding chemical irritant. Jack and Evan take the weapons, finish the crew, torch the van, and leave with the money. The friendship is broken. They are a lethal team.

Chapter 05

One Year Later

Florida swamp · A Cuban prison

They sink the cash in the swamp, locked in a cage and tied underwater. An alligator comes at them. They wrestle it down and later roast it. The agreement is simple: once they reach Cuba, they split for good.

A smuggler boat takes them south. Cuban border patrol stops them and throws them into prison. Evan says it outright. Jack destroyed his life. The two men who cannot stand each other are locked in together. In the States, Brown follows witness scraps to the marina where they vanished with the smugglers. Inside the prison, Jack and Evan understand they may never get out.

Chapter 06

One Year Later

The breakout · South America

A year inside has changed them. They are lean, tattooed, heads shaved. Detective Brown arrives with an escape he has spent that year building: the guard rotations, the weak points, the failures in the system. They are useless to him rotting in Cuba. He is addicted to the chase.

Two weeks later the alarms go off. Inmates turn on them. Guards close in. Jack risks his life to get Evan out, and they escape without a dime. At the extraction point Brown springs the other half of the plan. U.S. military units close in, until a local cartel hits the convoy. In the crossfire Jack and Evan take a vehicle, steal a prop plane, and crash-land in a South American field. A farming family that does not share their language feeds them and lets them clean up. Jack says they should part. He does not want to. He just saved Evan’s life. Evan says they can play it by ear. They talk about honest work, about going quiet, about starting over.

Epilogue

One Year Later

Get on the ground.

Cut to one year later. Masks on. Weapons up. They are inside a bank again. Cut to black.

Format and franchise

The rule

Every chapter opens exactly one year later. That cut delivers a complete, high-velocity crime thriller at feature length, about ninety pages, and it also breaks cleanly into six episodes. Nothing has to be rebuilt to make the limited series. A later season, or a later film, does not reboot the premise and does not clear the debt. It turns the calendar. One more year. The rule holds.
