Written by Tyler Forrest

Logline

Changing names, a marriage that cannot survive the money, a betrayal buried inside the loyalty, and a retired detective who refuses to let the case die.

The hook

The missing year is the thriller.

  1. One jobA small-town bank. Blanks in the guns. A getaway that barely deserves the name.
  2. One cutEvery chapter opens exactly one year later. The running in between is never shown.
  3. One huntA widowed detective, told to enjoy his retirement, decides the case is the rest of his life.

Jack and Evan have been a fact in each other’s lives since they were boys. Jack can talk a bad idea into sounding like freedom. Evan still believes a plan can save a family — a wife, a lost job, a baby on the way. On the road into a town of a hundred people, a black Buick sits too calmly in the mirror. Jack convinces him the way through is the front door of a bank.

They get away with it. That is the least interesting thing that happens.

One Year Later is the six years after the money. You do not watch them run. You arrive when the new names are already slipping, when the marriage has already turned, when the man who was told to retire has already decided not to. The audience pieces together the missing time the way the men do: from a scar, a child, a car that should not be there, a loyalty that feels like too much. Jack is hiding a night with Claire. Evan loves him like a brother and will learn how to blame him like an enemy. Claire leaves the life the robbery was supposed to protect. Detective Brown follows them across countries because the chase is the only purpose he has left.

Six chapters. Then the rule fires one more time.

Year One

The Robbery

LocationSmall Town

StakesGet out with the money. Stay unknown.

Jack talks his oldest friend into a small-town bank. The guns are loaded with blanks. They get out. A detective a month from retirement is told to let it go.

Read the full yearHide the full year

The chapter opens in the car, before anyone says the word bank. Jack and Evan have been friends their whole lives. The money is gone. Evan’s job is gone. Claire is pregnant, and the marriage is already coming apart. Something between Jack and Claire is in the car with them — felt, not explained. They are driving into a town of a hundred people, and a black Buick has been in the mirror too long.

Masks. Guns. A robbery they are making up as they go. It works, barely. Only afterward is it clear the guns were loaded with blanks. They burn the car in the desert, take the money in a second car, and disappear. Detective Brown stands in what they left: blanks, almost no faces, a scrap of a clue. His partner tells him he retires next month. Go enjoy your life.

Year Two

The Escape

LocationTropical Island

StakesKeep the life they stole — or go home to a baby.

False names, a bar, cash that spends like proof. Evan wants to go home. A widowed detective hears too much. The island makes them leave.

Read the full yearHide the full year

One year later they are other people on a tropical island, and for a moment it looks like they won. Jack buys the bar and spends like a man who got away with it. Evan sits out of the light. He wants Claire. He wants the baby, who is almost here. They are bad at the new names.

A widowed man drinks alone and talks about a dead wife to a bartender who isn’t listening — then he hears Jack say he robbed a bank, joking or not. He follows. He hears regret, and he sees the cash. He calls someone who tells him to let it go. He is retired. He does not let it go. The next day Jack’s fight with a group of tourists brings the island police. They are released and told to leave. Evan has been saying they have to get back anyway. They take the next boat. Detective Brown watches it go.

Year Three

The Chase

LocationBack Home / Boardwalk

StakesA family in the open, and a line they haven’t crossed.

A one-year-old. A marriage Claire is already leaving. The black Buick is back, and it is not the police. By the end of the night they are no longer only thieves, and Brown has seen it.

Read the full yearHide the full year

The year has already done its damage. Evan and Claire have a one-year-old and a marriage that will not survive this life. Claire wants out. Jack shows up at the worst moment. The child runs to him before running to Evan, and the room goes quiet around a question the film refuses to answer. Jack pulls Evan aside: the black Buick is back. He makes Evan take a gun. Evan tells him to leave.

Days later the same car follows them. Evan’s fear puts the gun in his hand in front of Claire, who has never seen it. Jack finds his own car broken open and a note asking where the money is. Brown comes to the house without a badge, because he doesn’t have one anymore. Claire shuts the door and still gives away too much. She asks for the badge. She lies. Evan hides — he knows the face from the island. Brown slips a tracker under the car. Claire tells Evan he cannot stay. She will go to her sister. He will take a motel.

The next night the house is dark. Brown breaks in and finds fake IDs and cash. He has them. Jack calls Evan to the south end of the boardwalk. The man in the Buick was supposed to get a warning. Jack fires. It isn’t enough. Evan kills the man to keep Jack alive. They are murderers now. Brown reaches the scene in time to see it, old badge out. They run.

Year Four

The Siege

LocationRemote Canadian Cabin

StakesEach other — then the men who came for a son.

Winter. New names. A payphone call to a wife who is already gone. Jack tells the truth he has been carrying. Before that fight can finish them, the dead man’s people arrive.

Read the full yearHide the full year

A remote cabin. Beards, long hair, names they have to think about before they answer to them. Evan and Claire are divorced. He calls her from a payphone and talks about the child, about how faithful she was, about going back. Jack has been too interested in that baby. He finally says it: he slept with Claire near the start of the marriage. They beat each other bloody. Evan has a gun on Jack when the trucks arrive.

The man they killed had a father. He wants the money, and he wants blood. Jack and Evan, who were just trying to destroy each other, fight as one unit. They bring down the man who came for revenge, turn the men who only came for cash, and get out with the money. They burn what they leave behind.

Year Five

All Is Lost

LocationFlorida Swamp & Cuban Prison

StakesSeparate. The border refuses.

The money goes into a swamp. Cuba was supposed to be where they split. A border does not care. A cell does the opposite of a goodbye. Brown is still following the water.

Read the full yearHide the full year

They hide the money in a Florida swamp and agree, once they reach Cuba, to part. A drug boat takes them south. The plan dies at the border. They are arrested. In the prison, Evan says what he has been saying sideways for years: Jack destroyed his life. They are still locked in the same place, with the same person.

Brown, still unofficial, retraces them — locals who almost recognize a face, a marina, the smugglers they left with. The chapter ends with Jack and Evan sure they may die in that cell.

Year Six

The Breakout

LocationBreakout & South America

StakesOut of the cage. Brown’s door is the only one.

Brown visits with a way out and a motive that isn’t mercy. The break is real. The freedom on the other side is a trap. They live through it, and spend one night talking about normal work.

Read the full yearHide the full year

A year later they are thinner, marked, shaved down by the prison. Brown comes to visit. He has spent the year on the guards’ shifts and the things in the prison that fail. He slips them a plan. They ask why he would help. Because they are no good to him locked up in Cuba. He is addicted to the chase. They don’t trust him. They have no other door.

Two weeks later the alarms sound. Other inmates turn. Guards close in. Jack risks the exit to pull Evan through, and gets him out. They come up with nothing, into a circle of American military — and Brown. It was a setup. As they are taken, a local cartel opens fire. In the crossfire they take a vehicle, then a prop plane. The landing is a field in South America, hard and off any map they had.

A farm family that doesn’t share their language feeds them and lets them wash. Jack says they should part. He doesn’t mean it. He just saved Evan’s life. Evan says they can play it by ear. They talk about ordinary jobs. About being done. For the length of that night, they agree.

Dossiers

Four people. Six years.

The risk

Jack

A charismatic addict of freedom, loyal in a way that feels like too much.

What he wants

A life that never clocks in. He charms every room and hides how afraid he is. He refuses ordinary work, and he is the one who makes a bank sound possible.

What he hides

He slept with Claire after she married Evan. The fierce loyalty is real, and it is also guilt. He carries it like a second gun until a cabin in Canada forces it into the open.

Across the years

Year 2Spends the island like a verdict he got away with.
Year 4Tells Evan the truth, then stands with him against the people who came for blood.
Year 6Pulls Evan through the breakout, and still can’t ask him to stay.

The plan

Evan

A loyal planner, desperate for a life that sits still.

What he wants

Less hungry than Jack, and always a half-step behind the life he was promised. Fired, with a baby coming, he agrees to the robbery because providing still feels like a moral act.

What binds him

He loves Jack like a brother and will blame him like the author of every bad year. By the time he has killed for him, the friendship is no longer a sentiment. It is a record.

Across the years

Year 2The island is a prize he can’t enjoy. He wants Claire, and a child who will not wait.
Year 3Home ends the marriage. He kills so Jack will live.
Year 5In a cell he says it plainly: Jack ruined his life. He still can’t get away from him.

The life

Claire

Evan’s wife. Once, briefly, Jack’s. Not a prize between them.

What she wants

The life the money was supposed to protect. A child raised somewhere that isn’t a motel, a fake name, or a man who flinches at cars.

What she carries

One night with Jack after the wedding. The film lets the question of the child stay in the room — a reach, a look — and does not settle it.

The hunt

Brown

A retired detective who refuses to let the case die.

What he wants

Purpose. Recently widowed, officially finished, he builds a case no one asked for. The hunt is how he stays a detective after the job and the marriage are both over.

What he becomes

Smart enough to connect impossible dots. Unofficial enough that even the people who know him want him to stop. Helping them out of a foreign prison is not mercy. A cage ends his story.

Across the years

Year 3Walks into their house without a badge, then onto a murder in time to see it.
Year 6Hands them the only way out, because he is addicted to the chase. The door is also a trap.

Format & series

A feature. Or six episodes. The same cut.

One storytelling rule holds either way: every chapter opens exactly one year later. The feature is a complete story. Each chapter can also stand as an episode.

In a feature, you feel the year as a cut. You lose twelve months, and you are trusted to know who they became.

The film

Six chapters, one sitting. The grammar is the jump. The epilogue does not soften the ending. It proves the rule. They said they were done. Then someone says get on the ground.

The series

The same six chapters, released as six episodes. Every cold open is the same promise: you are one year late. The missing time is why a viewer comes back. Nothing about the feature has to be broken to make the series. The chapters were built as both.

What continues. Another season, or another film, does not reboot the premise and does not clear the debt. It turns the calendar. One more year. The rule holds.
